### Guardrail changes: what each one does

- **Direct land holds the categories PR mode always holds for a human (#716).** `land_mode: direct` skips the merge decision. So a change in a category PR mode always holds for a human (review.yaml's L3 categories, CI config, the agents' own instructions, the harness's own controls, the odd paths, the repo's `merge.wait_categories`, and dependency manifests) could land at L0 to L2 with only the evaluator's approval. Such a change is now raised to L3, and the owner is asked, with the same reasons the merge policy gives in PR mode. The reasons read `high-risk: <category> (<paths>)` for a category, and `design: dependency manifests change (<paths>)` for a manifest. The floor is checked twice: once when the worker's change gets its level, and again just before the push, on the commit the land would push (R1182f). That second check covers what a `tests.yaml` `land.pre` step wrote, and uses the review config and `land_mode` in force at landing, so a change queued in PR mode or before a wait category was added is caught. If it finds a reason, nothing is pushed: each entry the owner hasn't approved goes back to the owner (L3), and the rest of its batch waits. Still not covered: a file an approved entry changed counts as approved only while it is exactly as at that entry's head, but which entry a pre-land step's other output belongs to can't be told, so an approval covers all of it. The rest of the merge decision still doesn't apply to a direct land: the evaluator's binding design flag, a new top-level module, the worker's protected claim (`claimedProtected`), the weakening and vacuity holds (#783, #784), the size limits and its other doubt reasons. Nor do its three switches: the instance's `policy.yaml` `auto_merge` kill switch, `review.yaml` `merge.auto`, and the auto-merge stop. Direct land checks only the emergency stop and the branch's protection; #808 (PR #1183) brings the stop and the policy switch to it, and whichever of #1182 and #1183 lands second corrects this sentence.
