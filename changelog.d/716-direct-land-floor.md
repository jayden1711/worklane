### Guardrail changes: what each one does

- **Direct land holds the categories PR mode always holds for a human (#716).** `land_mode: direct` skips the merge decision. So a change in a category PR mode always holds for a human (review.yaml's L3 categories, CI config, the agents' own instructions, the harness's own controls, the odd paths, the repo's `merge.wait_categories`, and dependency manifests) could land at L0 to L2 with only the evaluator's approval. Such a change is now raised to L3, and the owner is asked, with the same reasons the merge policy gives in PR mode. The reasons read `high-risk: <category> (<paths>)` for a category, and `design: dependency manifests change (<paths>)` for a manifest. The floor is checked twice: once when the worker's change gets its level, and again just before the push, on the commit the land would push (R1182f). That second check always runs, also when every entry is approved (R1182h). It covers what a `tests.yaml` `land.pre` step or a gate wrote, and uses the review config and `land_mode` in force at landing, so a change queued in PR mode or before a wait category was added is caught. A file counts as an entry's own only while it is exactly as at that entry's head: the same content, mode and type. One that changed is output, and no approval given at review time covers output. If the check finds a reason, nothing is pushed. Each entry the owner hasn't approved goes back to the owner (L3); with none, every entry does, unless the owner already approved those same reasons at landing. The rest of its batch waits. The question is asked before the entry's result is recorded, so a failure in between leaves it queued and asked again, never dropped. Which entry a pre-land step's output came from can't be told, so an owner's approval of it at landing covers that output for the whole batch; the question names it. The rest of the merge decision still doesn't apply to a direct land: the evaluator's binding design flag, a new top-level module, the worker's protected claim (`claimedProtected`), the weakening and vacuity holds (#783, #784), the size limits and its other doubt reasons. Nor do its three switches: the instance's `policy.yaml` `auto_merge` kill switch, `review.yaml` `merge.auto`, and the auto-merge stop. Direct land checks only the emergency stop and the branch's protection; #808 (PR #1183) brings the stop and the policy switch to it, and whichever of #1182 and #1183 lands second corrects this sentence.
